Workflow

Work moves through six stages and two gates. Agents do the work in each stage; designers, the PM and an engineer decide at the gates.

The workflow at a glance

Stages

Each stage has its own agents. An agent starts when the design item reaches the status that triggers it, and stops at a human check.

Between Explore and Design, designers make the anchor screens in Figma. Extend builds out from them and never works without them.

Gates

There are two gates. At each one, designers, the PM and an engineer decide together, using the evidence the agents have produced.

Stage statuses

The design item moves through these statuses in order. Some of them start an agent.

  1. Intake
  2. Discovery
  3. Discovery approved
  4. Exploring
  5. Shortlist agreed
  6. Anchors ready
  7. Designing
  8. Design signed off
  9. Prototyping and testing
  10. Handed over
Status
What it starts
Discovery Insight synthesises research and behaviour evidence.
Discovery approved Archive writes the discovery summary into the repo. Range starts on full-route items.
Shortlist agreed Set when the shortlist gate passes. Designers make the anchor screens.
Anchors ready Extend builds out the design from the anchors.
Design signed off Set when the design sign-off gate passes. Build starts the prototype.
Handed over Set on the feature by Relay . Engineering stories are ready.

Brief starts earlier, when product sets the feature to Ready for design. Map 's output is approved by designers, who then move the status to Discovery approved.

Fast track

Brief chooses the route for every feature and records why. The fast track goes from Discover straight to Design, skipping Explore and the shortlist gate.

Use the fast track only when all three are true:

  • there is no new flow
  • no new research is needed
  • only existing screens change

Otherwise the feature takes the full route.

Test loop

Prototypes are tested with people. Observe writes up the findings, and each finding says whether it goes back to Extend (a design change) or Build (a prototype change). Designers decide what changes. The loop repeats until testing is complete; then Relay hands over.

Cadence under scaled agile

Design runs one sprint ahead of engineering: engineering stories are ready only when the design is Handed over. The proposed rhythm:

When
What happens
Before PI planning Product sets the features expected in the next planning increment to Ready for design. Brief writes their UX briefs and chooses each route.
PI planning UX commits its sprint capacity against those features, using the routes to size them: fast-track items need less than full-route items.
Each sprint Design items move through their statuses. A feature planned for engineering in a sprint should reach Handed over by the end of the sprint before it.
Each week Gates are held at a fixed time, so a design item never waits more than a week for a decision. Archive runs its weekly check.
After release Relay checks the build against the design and the success measures. Findings feed the next planning increment.
