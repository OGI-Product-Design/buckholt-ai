Summary: where we stand on the NFRs

What Deloitte asked for

Requirement records for each product journey, each covering one quality attribute (performance, reliability, availability, security and so on), linked to a measurable outcome, the architecture that delivers it and the evidence today. Pilot a few journeys first, then align them to the target architecture.

What Jim asked for

Measure around six NFRs across both Mobius and Core, so the two products can be compared on the same terms and migration decisions rest on evidence.

Where we are

Evidence is in place for reliability and supportability on both products, and for performance and usability on Mobius only. Availability is missing on both, and security is partial. Mobius has written but unratified targets; Core has none. The architecture links Deloitte needs already exist in the Mobius wiki (21 principles, 78 decisions).

Insight: We can draft Deloitte records now for reliability and supportability on MTA, payments, quotes and renewals, where both data sources agree those journeys struggle. Availability, Core speed and Core usability need new instruments first: uptime reporting from Application Insights and a benchmark study on Core.

Types of Mobius customer

Mobius customers use the platform in three different ways, and this decides what each data source can see. LogRocket records only the Mobius screens; Power BI records every transaction, however it arrived.

UI-led

Staff do everything in the Mobius screens. Fully visible in LogRocket and Power BI.

Hybrid

Customers buy through the customer’s own website or app, which talks to Mobius through its APIs; staff then service policies in Mobius. LogRocket sees the staff servicing only; Power BI sees all the sales.

Headless

The customer’s own system calls Mobius through APIs and nobody uses the Mobius screens. Invisible to LogRocket; visible in Power BI once trading.

Not in baseline

Tenants that should not shape the Mobius picture yet: Pizza (staying on Core), Supercover (not trading), Markerstudy Pet (on hold), and Core customers migrating or onboarding (Footman James, Got You Covered, Stanhope Cooper / Everywhen).

Insight: Every Mobius figure from LogRocket describes UI users of the ten known live tenants (a saved segment built from the Mobius wiki tenant register). Hybrid and headless customers’ own-site sales are not in those figures, so LogRocket understates how much business flows through Mobius, while Power BI volumes include it.

How to read these figures
  • Core handles about 38 times as much work as Mobius, so compare rates and shares, not raw counts.
  • Core customer figures are by year (2025, and 2026 to date including part of November); Mobius customer figures are by month.
  • Core aborts and Mobius crashes both mean a user was stopped mid-task, but Core keeps only the last eight aborts per site and the source stopped refreshing in June 2026.
  • Only about 14% of support tickets are tagged to a product, so support figures are a sample. serious (Category A) (highest priority) is defined in the Mobius wiki as the system unavailable or severely degraded, or a material adverse impact on the customer’s business (recent examples: payments not processing, documents not sending, quoting or renewals not working). Categories B to F are not defined there. For Mobius, tickets raised at too low a category are given an “Expedite A/B” label rather than re-categorised, to avoid affecting SLAs, and SLAs pause while waiting for the customer or in UAT, so Category A counts and SLA-met rates understate urgent work.
  • Bug counts include bugs caught before release.
  • LogRocket counts sessions that reach a page; Core counts completed transactions.
  • Mobius transactions with no broker recorded are mostly Lawshield’s VW tenant (VW01) plus API-led customers that Power BI does not name; the “Supercover” tenant (SC01) now carries Lloyd Latchford Group.
  • Mobius journey rates were checked against how each LogRocket chart is built, and corrected where needed. "Client searches leading to a next step" includes searches that end in creating a new client. Quote completion now counts only the new business quote summary. Once a payment is started it almost always completes; the drop-off happens earlier, between the payment summary and choosing to pay.
  • Mobius "crash rate" counts sessions where a crash report is sent to LogRocket. None have been recorded in three months, which most likely means crash reporting is not switched on in Mobius, so 0% should not be read as proof of stability.
  • Every Mobius figure from LogRocket uses the saved segment “Known live Mobius customers (UI users)”: an include list of ten live tenants (IS01, BR01, VW01, LS01, JN01, UJ01, AL01, CC01, CE01, SC01) from the Mobius wiki register. Test, demo, migration, headless and non-baseline tenants are out by design. These figures describe Mobius screen use only; hybrid and headless customers’ API sales appear in Power BI but not in LogRocket.
  • Core site counts come from the UserInfo health log; a site that has not reported in 2026 may be dormant, decommissioned or simply not sending its log.
